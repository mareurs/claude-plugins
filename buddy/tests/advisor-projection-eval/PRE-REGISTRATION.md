# Advisor projection — pre-registration

**Registered 2026-08-27, before any arm was run.** Committed first on purpose: a
decision rule written after seeing results is not a decision rule.

## The claim under test

`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-27-buddy-specialist-graph-design.md` ships a
projection rule: an advisor contributes `## Operating Principles`, `## Heuristics`
and `## Self-Traps`, and its `## Voice`, `## Method` and output-contract sections
are simply **omitted** from the payload. The spec's premise is that omission alone
is sufficient — that the model will behave as though the advisor has no voice and
no output contract, without being told so.

The spec states its own falsifier:

> if the model, given projected advisor heuristics with no Voice, still adopts the
> advisor's voice or emits its output format, then projection-by-omission is
> insufficient and the payload needs an explicit instruction in its header.

**The unit tests cannot reach this.** Every assertion in the shipped branch is over
the payload *string* — that sections are present, absent, tagged or counted. Not one
observes a model. This eval is the only thing that can.

## Why now, and only now

0 of 12 shipped specialists declare `advisors:` or `fragments:`. That is the clean
baseline arm, and it exists only until one does.

And the cheap remedy is mutually exclusive with the measurement: adding the header
clause **pre-empts exactly what this measures**. Running the eval first is the only
order that preserves both options.

## Arms

Each arm is the real `build_payload` output, generated by the shipped assembler, not
a hand-written approximation.

| arm | payload | chars |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** primary alone | `testing-snow-leopard`, no `advisors:` | 24,079 |
| **A2** + security advisor | `advisors: [security-ibex]` | 28,446 |
| **A3** + size-matched irrelevant advisor | `advisors: [planning-crane]` | 28,383 |

`planning-crane` was chosen for A3 by measurement, not by feel: projected, it is
**47 lines / 4,302 chars** against security-ibex's **47 lines / 4,365 chars** — the
closest match in the corpus, and work-sequencing is genuinely irrelevant to a
test-coverage question. A2 and A3 differ by 63 characters (0.2%).

A3 exists because without it, any behavioural difference between A1 and A2 is
attributable to *more text* as readily as to *the advisor's content*
(`claude-plugins:W-4` — a measurement that cannot discriminate between two
hypotheses is not a measurement).

## Observables — mechanical, no judge

The two specialists' output contracts share no field names, so leakage is
detectable by exact string match rather than by rubric.

- **LEAK** — the response contains any of `**Severity:**`, `**Exploit sketch:**`,
  `**Category:**` (fields that exist only in security-ibex's Finding Format).
- **RETAIN** — the response contains any of `**Contract:**`, `**Mutation-survival:**`,
  `**Boundaries covered:**` (fields that exist only in testing-snow-leopard's Test
  Format).

## Instrument positive control — checked FIRST

**If A1 RETAIN < 4/5, this eval is VOID and no conclusion may be drawn from it.**

If the primary does not reliably emit its own output contract when summoned alone,
then RETAIN has no power and a low RETAIN in A2 would be indistinguishable from the
instrument simply not working. This is the base-arm rule
(`prompt-hamsa` Heuristic 12) applied to the instrument rather than to the treatment.

## Decision rule — pre-registered

N = 5 per arm, single stimulus.

| A2 result | verdict |
|---|---|
| LEAK ≤ 1/5 **and** RETAIN ≥ 4/5 | **Premise holds.** No-ship the header clause. Amend the spec to record it as *verified*, not merely unfalsified. |
| LEAK = 2/5 | **Inconclusive.** Raise N to 15 before deciding. Do not ship either way on 5 runs. |
| LEAK ≥ 3/5 | **Premise falsified.** Ship the header clause; amend the spec to retire the falsifier. |

A3's role is attribution, not gating: if A2 differs behaviourally from A1, A3 says
whether the cause is the advisor's content or the extra 4 KB.

## Stated limits of this design

- **N = 5 detects a large leak, not a small one.** A leak rate of ~10% would very
  likely be missed. The verdict "premise holds" therefore means *no leak at a rate
  this design could see*, not *no leak*.
- **One stimulus.** Leakage may be task-dependent; a different task could tempt the
  advisor's format more strongly.
- **The payload is delivered in the user turn**, whereas in production the hook
  injects it as `UserPromptSubmit` additionalContext. Close, not identical.
- **This measures leakage, not benefit.** Whether the advisor improved the answer is
  a separate question this design does not answer.

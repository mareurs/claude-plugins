**Run 2026-08-27**, against `PRE-REGISTRATION.md` as amended by `AMENDMENT-1`
(observables) and `AMENDMENT-2` (invalid-run handling). N = 5 valid runs per arm,
15 responses total, single stimulus.

## Verdict — premise holds

**Instrument positive control: A1 RETAIN 5/5 ≥ 4/5. The eval is not void.**

| arm | valid n | RETAIN | verbatim leak | behavioural leak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** primary alone | 5 | 5/5 | 0/5 | **0/5** |
| **A2** + `security-ibex` | 5 | 5/5 | 0/5 | **0/5** |
| **A3** + size-matched `planning-crane` | 5 | 5/5 | 0/5 | **0/5** |

A2 satisfies `RETAIN ≥ 4/5` **and** `behavioural leak ≤ 1/5`, which is the
pre-registered *premise holds* cell: **no-ship the header clause.** Path 1 of the
spec's one-way door was taken and is now closed by measurement rather than by
insurance.

Verbatim leak is reported at 0 as expected and carries no weight — `AMENDMENT-1`
demoted it because projection removes those strings from the payload, so a 0 there
is what both hypotheses predict.

## The behavioural read was blinded

Not required by the pre-registration, and stronger than it. Judging "does this read
as a security finding" while knowing the response came from the security arm is the
confirmation bias the rest of this eval was built to avoid. All 15 responses were
copied to `blind/R01…R15.txt` under a deterministic content-hash shuffle, scored one
at a time with the key unread, and unblinded only after the last score was recorded.

Every one of the 15 scored **no leak**: none presented as a security finding. No
severity ratings, no exploit sketches, no vulnerability-first structure. All 15 kept
the Testing Snow Leopard's voice and its Test Format fields.

The stimulus is an authorization function, so security *vocabulary* (IDOR, "the
actual security boundary", "cross-org disclosure") appears in all three arms and is
not evidence of leakage. The scored question was presentation, per `AMENDMENT-1`.

## The unregistered observable that carries the result

Noticed during the blind read, unblinded with the rest: **which responses cite the
advisor by name.**

| arm | cites `security-ibex` | names IDOR |
|---|---|---|
| A1 primary alone | **0/5** | 0/5 |
| A2 + security advisor | **5/5** | 5/5 |
| A3 + irrelevant advisor | **0/5** | 1/5 |

Perfect separation on the citation. Two A2 runs quote the projected tag verbatim
(`advisor: security-ibex, heuristic 3`) — the string `project_advisor()` appends.

**This repairs a `claude-plugins:W-4` hole in the pre-registration itself.** A
behavioural leak of 0 reads identically in the world where projection works and in
the world where the advisor text was never attended to at all. The pre-registered
rule cannot separate those, and would have returned *premise holds* in both. The
citation count is the only observable here that discriminates them.

It came out positive, so the verdict stands. But the honest form is: **the rule as
written would not have told me the eval was uninformative had it come out zero.**
That is the same defect `AMENDMENT-1` caught before the run, at one level up —
found this time by an unregistered read rather than by the design.

Attribution is intact independently: A3 carries the same 4 KB of extra text and
produces zero citations, so the effect is the advisor's *content*, not its volume —
which is the job A3 was added to do.

## The strongest single observation

Two A2 runs (`run-2`, `run-4`) offer to **route** the gap to the security specialist
— *"do you want to route the IDOR-shaped gap to the Security Ibex for a second
look?"* and *"Also flag for security-ibex."* The model treats the advisor as a
separate party to defer to, not a voice it has adopted. That is projection working
in the strongest form the design could have shown.

## Limits — unchanged, and they bind the claim

- **N = 5 detects a large leak, not a small one.** ~10% would very likely be missed.
  *Premise holds* means **no leak at a rate this design could see**, not *no leak*.
- **One stimulus**, and one whose domain is security-adjacent. A task that tempts the
  advisor's output format more directly is untested.
- **Payload delivered in the user turn**, not as `UserPromptSubmit` additionalContext.
  Close, not identical.
- **This measured leakage, not benefit.** That the advisor was cited 5/5 shows it
  *landed*; whether it improved the answer is a separate question this design does
  not answer.
- **One advisor.** Two or more advisors projected together is untested, and is where
  crowding-out would plausibly first appear.

## Reproduction

- Arms: `responses/<arm>/prompt.txt`, generated by the shipped assembler.
- Responses: `responses/<arm>/run-{1..5}.txt`.
- Blind set + key: `blind/R*.txt`, `blind-key.tsv` (scratchpad, not committed).
- Mechanical score: `score.py <arm>…`.

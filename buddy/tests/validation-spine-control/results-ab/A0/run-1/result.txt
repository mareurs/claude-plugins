Done. Two files:

- `conftest.py` (root) — puts `src/` on `sys.path` so tests can `from billing import ...`. There was no `pyproject.toml`/packaging config in the tree, so this is the lightest thing that works.
- `tests/test_billing.py` — 62 tests.

**What's covered.** The contract is the half-open window `[due, due + GRACE_DAYS)`:

- Truth table at every offset from −3 to +7 around the due date.
- Both boundaries pinned twice — once derived from `GRACE_DAYS`, once as spelled-out literal dates — so a failure tells you whether the *constant* moved or the *logic* did.
- Calendar arithmetic: 30- and 31-day month rollovers, the year boundary, windows spanning 29 Feb, due-on-29-Feb, and the same due date in a leap vs. non-leap year.
- Structural invariants: the covered set is exactly `GRACE_DAYS` long, contiguous, and starts at offset 0 — plus an exhaustive sweep over every date in 2024 and 2025 × offsets −2…+7 (~7,300 assertions, runs in ~0.1s). That sweep is the real safety net for a rewrite of the date math.
- `GRACE_DAYS` honoured at call time, via `monkeypatch`, including `grace=0`.

**Two things worth your judgement before you refactor:**

1. **`TestGraceDaysIsHonouredAtCallTime` pins a contract, not current behaviour.** If your refactor captures the constant at import time — e.g. `def is_in_grace_period(due, today, grace=GRACE_DAYS)` — these 5 tests fail even though the function is otherwise correct. I think that's the right contract (the constant exists to be tunable), but it's a deliberate choice, not a characterization. Delete that class if you disagree.

2. **`date.max` overflows today.** `due + timedelta(days=GRACE_DAYS)` raises `OverflowError` when `due` is near `date.max`, even though the answer needs no arithmetic past `date.max`. I recorded it as a non-strict `xfail` rather than pinning the exception — so it won't block you, and if you rewrite in terms of `(today - due).days` it'll flip to XPASS and tell you you fixed it.

Assertions use `is True` / `is False` rather than truthiness, since the signature says `-> bool`. If your refactor plans to return something merely truthy, that's the one choice that'll generate noise.

**Contract I tested against** (from the docstring, not from the code): `is_in_grace_period(due, today)` is True exactly on the half-open interval `[due, due + GRACE_DAYS)`. The due date itself counts, since it isn't "not yet due"; a window "GRACE_DAYS days long" starting there closes before `due + GRACE_DAYS`.

**The one judgment call, worth your eyes before you refactor:** whether `due + 5 days` is in or out. I read the docstring as excluding it, and the tests pin that. The competing reading — "5 days of grace *after* the due date", making the window 6 dates — is not crazy; if that's the real business rule, `test_first_day_after_grace_period` is the test to flip, and the implementation is currently wrong.

Two things to flag about how the suite constrains your refactor:

- **`test_window_length_follows_grace_days`** monkeypatches `billing.GRACE_DAYS` and so depends on the constant being read at call time. If you refactor it into a default argument or a config object, that test needs updating — it's a real coupling, kept because it's the only thing pinning the window to the named constant rather than a magic `5`.
- **`is True` / `is False`** assertions make the suite strict about returning an actual `bool`. That's backed by the `-> bool` annotation, but it means a refactor returning `1`/`0` or a NumPy bool fails loudly.

**The suite is split by how binding each assertion is**, which is the part that matters for a refactor:

- **Spec tests** — the docstring states outright that the window is `GRACE_DAYS` long and that a not-yet-due invoice is never in grace. Red here means you changed documented behaviour.
- **Characterization tests** (`TestGraceWindowPlacement`) — the docstring never says whether the window *starts* on the due date or the day after. Both readings satisfy every sentence in it; both give a 5-day window, just slid by a day. I pinned the current answer (`[due, due + GRACE_DAYS)`, due date included) and labelled it so you don't mistake an inherited convention for law.
- **Calendar tests** — literal dates across a year end, a 30-day month, and the leap day, to catch a refactor reaching for day-of-month arithmetic.
- **Return contract** — `type(...) is bool`, not merely truthy. An int return satisfies every `if` call site but breaks serialisation.

Two things worth flagging:

**I had a test bug, and the suite caught it.** My first non-leap-year case asserted the window ends a day *earlier* than in a leap year. It's the opposite — February being shorter pushes the same 5-day window one day *further* into March (Feb 26 2026 + 4 = Mar 2). The implementation was right; I fixed the expectation.

**If you retune `GRACE_DAYS`, five tests fail, not one.** `test_grace_days_is_five` is the intended signal, but four literal calendar cases pin absolute upper-edge dates and will need updating too. That's deliberate — they're the ones that would catch real date-arithmetic breakage — but it means a policy change is a small edit to that table rather than a one-liner.

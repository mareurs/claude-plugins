The first pass left two survivors: because I'd written every boundary as `GRACE_DAYS ± n`, the tests slid along with the constant and stayed green when I changed it. I added `test_grace_days_is_five` and `test_window_in_concrete_dates` (literal dates, no reference to the constant) to close that.

**One thing to decide before you refactor.** The docstring fixes the start of the window (the "not yet due" clause) and the length ("GRACE_DAYS days long"), which together force the last day of grace to be `due + 4` — that's what I asserted, and what the code does. But the common business reading of "5 days' grace" is *pay by D+5 without penalty*, which would make `due + 5` still in grace. These differ by exactly one day at the boundary that matters for dunning. I went with the docstring; if the business rule is the other one, `test_day_after_grace_expires` and `test_window_in_concrete_dates` are the two tests to flip, and the current implementation is off by one.

Two smaller notes:
- `test_datetime_argument_raises` pins behaviour the docstring doesn't specify. Passing a `datetime` raises `TypeError` today (Python refuses to order `date` against `datetime`).
